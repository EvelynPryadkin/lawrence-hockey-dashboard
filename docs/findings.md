# What the game data shows

This analysis covers 138 Lawrence women's hockey games from 2020–21 through 2025–26. The data was collected from Lawrence Athletics on September 24–25, 2026. Goals and shots reconcile to the published season totals. Some power-play opportunity counts are missing or disagree with the season summaries, as described below.

These are team-level comparisons. They do not account for opponent strength, player availability, ice time, or shot quality. The questions at the end of each finding are suggestions for a coach to investigate, not conclusions from game film or conversations with the team.

## 1. Scoring fell faster than shots in 2025–26

| Season | Games | Goals | Shots on goal | Goals per game | Shots per game | Shooting percentage |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2024–25 | 27 | 37 | 492 | 1.37 | 18.22 | 7.52% |
| 2025–26 | 25 | 21 | 378 | 0.84 | 15.12 | 5.56% |

Lawrence scored 0.53 fewer goals per game in 2025–26, a 38.7% decline. Shots per game fell by 3.10, or 17.0%. The share of shots that became goals also fell, from 37/492 to 21/378: a decline of 1.96 percentage points. Both fewer shots and a lower shooting percentage appear in the scoring decline. The percentages use the unrounded rates, so the calculations may differ slightly from arithmetic on the displayed values.

Per-game averages account for the different schedule lengths, but they do not adjust for who Lawrence played. Shots on goal also leave out missed and blocked attempts. Without shot locations or video, this cannot tell us whether the team created fewer strong chances, faced better goaltending, or experienced normal scoring variation.

**Question for a coach:** Against opponents faced in both seasons, did Lawrence create fewer shots from dangerous areas, or did similar chances produce fewer goals?

Evidence: [season comparison results](analysis/season_over_season.csv), generated by `season_over_season` in [the SQL file](../sql/analysis.sql).

## 2. Home scoring was slightly lower in the latest season

| 2025–26 venue | Games | Goals | Shots on goal | Goals per game | Shots per game |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Home | 13 | 10 | 195 | 0.77 | 15.00 |
| Away | 12 | 11 | 183 | 0.92 | 15.25 |

Away scoring was 0.15 goals per game higher, while the difference in shots was only 0.25 per game. That is a small descriptive difference across 25 games, not evidence that playing away improves scoring. The pattern also changed from 2024–25, when Lawrence averaged 1.44 goals at home across nine games and 1.35 away across 17 games. That season's one neutral-site game stays separate.

The schedules were different at each venue. In 2025–26, Lawrence played Adrian twice at home and Trine twice away, with no games against those opponents at the other venue. Opponent mix, game dates, and sample size make a simple home-versus-away comparison hard to interpret.

**Question for a coach:** Does the difference remain when reviewing only the six opponents Lawrence faced at both venues in 2025–26?

Evidence: [season and venue results](analysis/season_venue_comparison.csv) and [opponent counts by venue](analysis/opponent_comparison.csv). The queries keep season and venue visible so an archive-wide average does not hide these differences.

## 3. Penalty kill declined, but the source totals need a footnote

| Season | Games with opponent opportunities | Opponent power-play goals | Opponent opportunities from box scores | Penalty kill |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2024–25 | 27 of 27 | 16 | 92 | 82.61% |
| 2025–26 | 25 of 25 | 22 | 92 | 76.09% |

Using the recorded box scores, penalty kill fell by 6.52 percentage points. Both seasons have 92 opponent opportunities, while goals allowed on those opportunities increased from 16 to 22. The rate is `100 × (1 − goals allowed / opportunities)`, calculated from season totals.

The published season summaries list 91 opponent opportunities in each season. Using those denominators gives 82.42% and 75.82%, respectively. The direction of the change is the same under either source, but the exact rates differ. This project keeps the observed box-score counts and records the conflict instead of silently changing a game.

Lawrence's own power play converted 5 of 78 opportunities in 2025–26, or 6.41%, with counts available for all 25 games. A full-season comparison with 2024–25 is unavailable: one of that season's 27 Lawrence opportunity counts is missing, and the 74 known opportunities already exceed the season summary's 72. Dividing all four power-play goals by that incomplete denominator would mix different coverage, so the dashboard shows N/A and SQL returns NULL.

Across the full archive, 23 games have at least one missing opportunity count. Each opportunity column has 22 missing values. The recorded 2021–22 power-play goal conflict is also unresolved. These gaps prevent a reliable six-season power-play or penalty-kill rate from the included game records.

**Question for a coach:** What changed in the penalty-kill possessions that ended in goals in 2025–26, and can the conflicting opportunity totals be reconciled with the official game sheets?

Evidence: [special-teams results and coverage](analysis/special_teams_coverage.csv), [source notes](sources.md), and [the season audit](../data/reference/season_audit.json).

## Reproduce the numbers

Run `python -m src.run_analysis` from the project root. It validates the raw CSV and rebuilds the result files linked above. [The SQL guide](sql-guide.md) explains the calculations, including a five-game rolling average for reviewing changes within a season. The [result manifest](analysis/manifest.json) records file hashes so changes to the input or SQL can be identified. The text here describes the September 2026 snapshot and should be reviewed if the data changes.
